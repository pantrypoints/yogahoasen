Kiirtan is a form of musical meditation, popular in Indian spiritual traditions and particularly emphasized in the Ananda Marga. It's not just about singing or repeatedly chanting mantras, but also a process of purifying the mind, opening up energy, and nurturing love within each individual.

## Posture and Gestures in Kiirtan

In Kiirtan, practitioners typically stand upright, raising both hands above their heads, symbolizing the opening of the crown chakra (Sahasrara), receiving cosmic energy and connecting with inner strength. The rhythm is often accompanied by music (drums, guitar, clapping, etc.) or gentle body movements, allowing energy to flow naturally and harmoniously.

## Common Mantra in the Ananda Marga

“Baba Nam Kevalam” (meaning: “Love is all that exists”). When chanting this mantra, the mind gradually leaves behind daily anxieties and turns towards boundless love and peace.

## Benefits of Kiirtan

- Purifies the mind, making it easier for practitioners to delve into meditation.

- Awakens joy, dispels anxiety and negative emotions.

- Connects spiritually, bringing the individual into alignment with universal consciousness.

- Enhances community energy, as everyone sings the mantra together in a circle of love.

## Kiirtan and Meditation

In Ananda Marga, Kiirtan is often practiced before meditation (sadhana). If Kiirtan is the gateway, then meditation is the step into the depths of the inner self. This combination makes the meditation process easier, more natural, and more complete.

👉 Kiirtan is not just singing, but a sacred ritual – where music, energy, and love blend, guiding people to peace and connection with the universe.
