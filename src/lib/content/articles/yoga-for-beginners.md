"I'm not flexible enough for yoga." This is the most common thing we hear from people who have never tried yoga. And it is wonderfully, completely backwards.

Yoga is not a test of flexibility. It is a practice that builds flexibility — along with strength, balance, clarity, and calm — over time. You do not need to touch your toes. You need only a willingness to begin.

## What to Expect in Your First Class

A typical beginner's yoga class at Yoga Hoa Sen lasts 60–75 minutes and moves through several phases:

**Centering (5 minutes):** You arrive on your mat, sit quietly, and begin to arrive — in body and mind. The teacher may guide a brief breathwork exercise.

**Warm-up (10–15 minutes):** Gentle movement to awaken the body — cat-cow, neck rolls, hip circles.

**Standing postures (20 minutes):** Mountain pose, warrior sequences, tree pose. These build strength and teach alignment.

**Floor postures (15 minutes):** Seated forward folds, twists, and hip openers. This is where flexibility gradually develops.

**Savasana (5–10 minutes):** The final pose. You lie still and allow the practice to integrate. Do not skip this. Many students consider savasana the most important part.

## Common Beginner Worries (and Why They're Fine)

**"I'll fall over in balance poses."** Yes, you probably will. So does everyone. Falling is part of learning. The floor is close. We laugh.

**"I'll be the least flexible person there."** You may be. This matters not at all. Every person in the room was a beginner once.

**"I don't know what the teacher means by 'root down through your sitting bones.'"** Neither did any of us on day one. These cues become intuitive with time. Ask the teacher after class.

**"I have an old injury."** Always inform the teacher before class. Yoga is infinitely adaptable. A good teacher will offer modifications so you can practice safely.

## Your First Week

Come to class three times in your first week if possible. Consistency in the early weeks allows your body to begin adapting and helps the language and rhythm of yoga become familiar.

After class, you may feel tender. This is normal muscle adaptation. A warm bath with Epsom salts is excellent.

Notice what happens to your sleep, your energy, and your mood in the days between classes.

## One Piece of Advice

Leave your expectations at the door. Don't compare your body to the person on the next mat. Don't compare your body today to your body last year.

Simply breathe, move, and notice. That is enough. That is yoga.
